LiDAR is the right tier to attack first. Its walls (25/26 within 1%) and ceilings (max error 0.4 cm) show that the geometry underneath is accurate, so an opening error of 2 to 6 cm is not sensor noise. It comes from how the opening detector turns that geometry into edges. On the video and photo tiers, opening errors come mainly from wall placement and depth error, which this fix does not touch.

## 2. Root-cause hypothesis and evidence

**Hypothesis:** the edge cell at each jamb is systematically voted closed. Openings are found on a 2 cm (along the wall) × 4 cm grid on the wall plane. A cell counts as open if `through_rays ≥ 2·plane_hits + 1`. The jamb reveal, the short surface between the wall faces at the edge of the opening, lies exactly at the true edge and within the ±3 cm plane-hit tolerance. Every edge cell therefore receives reveal hits and goes closed, so each side loses about one cell (2 cm). The measured width is also quantised to whole cells.

**Evidence** (`scripts/diag_openings.py` splits each width error into its two edges):
- LiDAR, 28 edges across 3 captures: **mean −1.7 cm per edge, median −2.0 cm (one cell)**, with **80–100% of edges inward** (opening too narrow) and at most 10% outward.
- The error is symmetric across both edges, not a centre shift. So it is not a wall-placement or pose error: those would move both edges the same way.
- All 14 LiDAR widths are narrow (−2 to −6 cm), and none is wide. Random noise would produce both signs, so this is a systematic bias.
- Widths come out in exact multiples of the 2 cm cell (0.760, 0.820, 0.840, 1.360 …), which is the quantisation signature.

**What would falsify it:** if sub-cell edge localisation leaves the per-edge mean below −1 cm, the hypothesis is wrong, and the loss comes from somewhere else (for example, morphological opening of the mask).

## 3. Fix and predicted number

**Fix:** sub-cell jamb localisation (`_refine_jambs` in `propscan/geometry/structure.py`; toggled by `geometry.jamb_refine`).

For each coarse edge, take the raw evidence in a ±6 cm window along the wall, within the opening's vertical band:
- wall-face hits, which lie in the wall plane;
- through-ray crossing points, where rays pass the wall plane.

Then place the edge at the position that best separates the two sets: the decision stump that maximises the hits on the wall side plus the crossings on the opening side, at 5 mm resolution. Reveal hits sit *at* the edge, so they no longer pull it inward. This uses the measurements directly instead of a cell vote.
